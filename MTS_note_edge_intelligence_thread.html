00THE PREMISE

A memory problem, not a coverage problem

Reading the coverage & critic articles, most feedback treats MTS as a distribution problem: reach, speed, virality. Coming from cognitive science and content distribution, I think that's a misdiagnosis. MTS relies on X, which already delivers information faster than any show — but X, by design, doesn't prioritise consolidation, so it never converts into understanding you keep.

Sensemaking, while MTS is on X, is a salience problem — not a distribution one.

01MEDIA GROWTH

Two verticals worth owning

Before owning AI, crypto, or geopolitics, you'd prioritize 1–2 realms. I looked for real-time firehoses people are emotionally invested in and can't parse alone. Two stand out — and they're live keywords, so drop them into the graph:

Where AI was three years ago — accelerating, emotionally charged, barely covered by anyone who can read the science well enough to separate breakthrough from hype. I'm a neuro-nerd, but that's not why I pitch it; it's why MTS could own the vertical before it's contested.

  • Format: Weekly anchor + live coverage on launch / funding / regulation days

02. Predictionsclick me to add me as a node →

Most channels compete with prediction markets; MTS should interrogate them. Why is this market priced this way? What does the crowd know that the headlines don't? Your audience already trusts a Polymarket price over a pundit — so make the market the protagonist and add the reasoning behind the move.

  • Format: Daily/rapid segment tied to live Kalshi & Polymarket movement
  • Book: The traders moving the line, the analysts, the investors
  • Firehose? Yes — markets move continuously and are real-time friendly

02PRODUCT GROWTH

A Bloomberg for meaning, not just data

When you asked which company I think is cool, I named Flora. When I asked what product growth looks like, you described a Bloomberg-esque tool that lets people build meaning from what they gather. So borrow Flora's node-canvas and point it at news: every story becomes a node, and the connections are the product.

Why? Memory consolidates through connection — the act of linking two things is what retains them. That makes a node-graph the right cognitive architecture for sensemaking. Flora never made that argument; it's time distribution does.

It also solves the empty-canvas trap. The show does the first pass — hosts publish each day's stories with connections drawn. The user forks it, extends the threads they care about, and builds a view over time, with a light AI layer suggesting links.
