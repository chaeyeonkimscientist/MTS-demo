A NOTE ON WHERE I'D BUILD FOR MTS

Making Tidings Stick (MTS!)

The information already arrives. The job is making it stick.

Hi Brent! You said you want to grow MTS on two sides:

  • 1 · Media growth — incubate shows across new verticals, generating raw materials
  • 2 · Product growth — build an artifact people can use the media materials with

I think these goals are interconnected, and there is one concrete product that brings the two together. I'll elaborate below.

The premise: a memory problem, not a coverage problem

Upon reading some news coverage & critic articles, I realized that most feedback treats MTS as a distribution problem: reach, speed, and virality.

Coming from cognitive science and content distribution, I think this is a misdiagnosis. MTS relies on X, which already delivers the information faster than any show. X, by its product design, does not prioritise consolidation, so information does not convert into understanding you keep.

Thus sensemaking — whilst MTS is using X — is more of a salience problem, less a distribution one.

First, media growth

Before you own coverage on AI, crypto, geopolitics etc., you'd have to prioritize 1–2 realms. I did a quick research on real-time firehoses people are emotionally invested in and can't parse alone. Here are two.

01. Neurotech

The AI market 3 years ago was accelerating fast, emotionally charged, and almost no one was covering it. Brain–computer interfaces, neural wearables, the fight over neural-data rights and "cognitive liberty" — almost no one covering it can actually read the science well enough to separate breakthrough from hype.

I'm a neuro-nerd, but that's not the reason why I pitch this. But because I am one, I think MTS should own the vertical before it's contested.

  • Format: Weekly anchor show + live coverage on launch/funding/regulation days
  • Book: Neuralink / Merge / Precision founders, neuroethicists, the FDA-watchers, the skeptics. Don't shy away from hosting academics — they'll call out the BS.
  • Firehose? Yes, and accelerating, with a built-in ethics fault line that drives debate

On credibility: this segment should counter MTS's current tech panel — emphasize critique and calling out the BS in real time. Most media distribution channels are scared to.

02. Predictions

Most distribution channels compete with prediction markets, but I think MTS should interrogate them. The show would ask: why is this market priced this way? What does the crowd know that the headlines don't? Where is the crowd wrong, and what bias is baked into the number? Your audience already trusts a Polymarket price over a pundit — so make the market the protagonist and add the reasoning behind the move.

  • Format: Daily/rapid segment tied to live Kalshi & Polymarket movement
  • Book: The traders moving the line, the analysts, and investors
  • Firehose? Yes, markets move continuously and are real-time friendly

Second, a product that takes it from there

When you asked which company I think is cool, I name-dropped Flora. And when I asked what growth looks like from the product side, you described a Bloomberg-esque product that has people build meaning from the information they got.

To build a product that lets people retain the information from shows, I'd borrow the shape from Flora's node-canvas and point it at news instead of images. Every story becomes a node. (That's the panel on your right — it's EP 83 as a graph. Hover a node for what it is, drag it anywhere, click any two to link them. The corner assistant suggests connections based on what's shipping next in tech.)

  1. show airs
  2. →
  3. stories become nodes
  4. →
  5. connections drawn
  6. →
  7. a graph you keep

Why? Memory consolidates through connection — the act of linking two pieces of information retains them, which makes it great cognitive architecture for sensemaking. Flora never made that argument, and it's time distribution does.

And it solves the empty-canvas trap. Most people won't build a graph from scratch, so the show does the first pass. Hosts and producers publish each day's stories with the connections already drawn. The user forks it, extends the threads they care about, and builds a view over time with a light AI layer suggesting links (on-canvas assistance). This acts as a custom catalog for the information overload of an always-on stream.

The through-line: the two sides are one pipeline

This product generates the pipeline: shows generate the graph → the graph makes the show durable and personal. For MTS, what you cover determines what the product can connect, and how people use the product tells you what to cover next.
